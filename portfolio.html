TradeZone E-Commerce SQL Analysis

PostgreSQLSQLData CleaningData Validation
Overview

A comprehensive SQL analysis of TradeZone, a fast-growing Nigerian e-commerce platform operating across Lagos, Abuja, Kano, Port Harcourt and Ibadan. The project identifies operational bottlenecks masking explosive 150% year-over-year growth and delivers strategic recommendations to improve customer retention and regional expansion.

Problem Statement

TradeZone had explosive top-line growth, but underlying problems threatened sustainability: declining customer retention driven by regional fulfillment delays, payment infrastructure gaps, and quality control issues on high-value products. Leadership needed data-driven insight to convert one-time buyers into repeat customers.

What I Did
  • Cleaned two years (2023–2024) of transactional data — handled NULL values, duplicates, inconsistent city formatting, and validated order totals against line items
  • Validated review ratings, product prices and discount percentages
  • Wrote 8 targeted SQL queries covering customer acquisition, product performance, seller efficiency, revenue trends, payment preferences, and quality metrics
Key Findings
  • Regional fulfillment crisis: Lagos sellers deliver in 2–3 hours; Kano and Port Harcourt exceed 72 hours — 30-day conversion is 12% in Lagos vs under 5% in Kano
  • High-value product quality risk: products rated below 3.0 average ₦88,000, higher than better-rated products, threatening brand perception on expensive items
  • Digital payment gap: Lagos runs 45% card payments; Kano is 85% cash-on-delivery with near-zero mobile money, limiting scalability in northern markets
Recommendations
  • Pilot a 24-hour dispatch SLA with top Kano sellers via a 3PL partner — projected to cut delivery time from 72 to 36 hours and lift repeat purchase rate from 5% to 9%
  • Introduce a verified quality badge for the top 20 high-revenue, low-rated products — projected to cut negative reviews by 15% within 60–90 days

Nigeria 2026 Federal Budget Analysis

Power BIExcelData ExtractionPython
Overview

A comprehensive analysis of Nigeria's ₦51.59 trillion 2026 Federal Government Budget, focused on the Security, Health and Education sectors, revealing spending patterns, resource allocation priorities, and their real-world implications for Nigerian citizens.

Problem Statement

Nigeria's federal budget is complex and often misunderstood — citizens struggle to see how ₦51.59 trillion in public funds is distributed and spent. This project translates official government appropriation data into transparent, meaningful insight.

What I Did
  • Extracted data directly from the official 2026 Federal Government Appropriation Bill published by the Budget Office of the Federation — no projections or estimates used
  • Manually structured PDF budget data into clean Excel workbooks, fixing formatting inconsistencies, NaN values and agency codes
  • Validated numerical columns and sector totals against source documents
  • Built an interactive Power BI dashboard covering 56 MDAs
Key Findings
  • 50% of the budget (₦25.88 trillion) is committed to debt servicing and statutory transfers before any ministry operates
  • Security receives 2.4× more funding than Education and 2.7× more than Health
  • 64% of operational spending goes to salaries and overhead — only 36% funds actual development projects
  • Teaching hospitals receive roughly a third of what their parent ministry headquarters costs to run

Health For Her: Maternal Mortality Analysis in Africa

ExcelPower BIData Cleaning
Overview

Analysis and visualization of maternal mortality data across African countries between 2012 and 2022, supporting Sustainable Development Goal 3.1 — reducing the global maternal mortality ratio to under 70 per 100,000 live births.

Objective
  • Understand maternal mortality trends across regions in Africa
  • Investigate the correlation between maternal health outcomes and health expenditure
  • Identify high-risk areas and inform targeted interventions
What I Did

Sourced data from World Bank Open Data covering maternal mortality ratio, number of maternal deaths, lifetime risk, and health expenditure (% of GDP). Filtered for African countries, removed duplicates, managed missing values, and merged multiple datasets into one clean file, then built Power BI visualizations including an MMR trend line, regional breakdowns, and top/bottom country comparisons.

Key Insights
  • Over 2 million maternal deaths recorded between 2012 and 2022
  • West and East Africa bear the highest burden of maternal deaths
  • Despite high health investment in some regions, outcomes remain poor — especially in Central Africa
  • Regional variation is significant, meaning targeted (not blanket) strategies are needed

Amazon Product Review Analysis

ExcelPivot TablesDashboarding
Overview

An analysis of 1,465 Amazon product listings and their reviews, built to help sellers make informed decisions on pricing, marketing and inventory based on product performance and customer sentiment.

What I Did
  • Cleaned the dataset — removed duplicates and blank rows, split comma-separated fields into structured columns, standardized price and percentage formats
  • Created calculated columns for potential revenue and rating × review count
  • Built pivot tables and an interactive dashboard with KPIs, slicers and category filters
Key Findings
  • Electronics, Home & Kitchen, and Computers & Accessories are the top three categories by volume
  • 583 products carry a discount of 50% or more, with the deepest discounts (up to 94%) in Computers & Accessories
  • Discount level and rating show only a weak negative correlation (−0.145) — heavier discounting doesn't reliably hurt ratings
  • Most products are rated between 4.0 and 4.4, reflecting broadly high customer satisfaction
Recommendations
  • Focus marketing on high-performing categories like Electronics
  • Monitor and consider relisting products rated below 3 stars
  • Keep price points competitive in the ₹200–₹500 range for volume

Kultra Mega Stores SQL Analysis

SQL ServerBusiness Insights
Overview

An analysis of historical order data (2009–2012) from Kultra Mega Stores, a Lagos-based retailer of office supplies and furniture, supporting strategic decision-making for the company's Abuja division.

What I Did

Queried two structured datasets (order/sales details and return status) in SQL Server to extract insights on product performance, customer segments, and shipping methods.

Key Findings
  • Technology was the highest-selling category, at ₦5,984,248.18 in total sales
  • Top regions by sales: West, Ontario, Prairie — bottom regions: Yukon, Northwest Territories, Nunavut
  • Dennis Kane was the top small-business customer by sales; Adam Hart placed the most corporate orders (18) between 2009–2012
  • Express Air shipping was generally reserved for Critical/High priority orders, though some Medium/Low priority orders also used it — a cost-optimization opportunity
Recommendations
  • Re-engage low-performing customers through loyalty programs and personalized offers
  • Audit shipping costs and realign shipping modes with order priority
  • Invest further in high-performing segments such as Technology and Corporate customers
